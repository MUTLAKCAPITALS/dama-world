## Design rules taken from them

1. **Covered alley.** A timber pergola spans the alley. It is carried on wooden posts on one side and corbelled ledgers on the facades on the other. Battens keep the shade dappled, not solid.
2. **Raised shops on both sides.** Every shop sill is 20–35 cm above the paving.
3. **Ground as a designed pattern, not noise:**
   - grey square pavers on one 40 cm grid;
   - beige bands along each facade and across the alley at every pergola post;
   - circular medallions on the centre line;
   - slot drains under the roof edges where rain falls.
   - Tone variation is held to a few percent per stone. There is no random grey/beige mixing.
4. **Mixed building ages and heights:**
   - a two-storey merchant building (the approved hero);
   - a single-storey arcade (Building B);
   - a two-storey merchant house with a timber roshan, joined to a single-storey shop row (Building C).
5. **Same construction language everywhere:** plaster over stone, چندل pole ends, timber lintels, rain spouts, iron bars, studded doors. The plaster tone, the paint colours and the trades change from building to building.
6. **Lived-in cues:** goods outside shops, signboards, lanterns, stains under every spout and pole end, planters, benches.

## Not used (no source for them in this souq)
Mashrabiya screens on shopfronts, crenellations, glazed tiles on façades, modern glass façades.
